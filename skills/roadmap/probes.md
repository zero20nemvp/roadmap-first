# Probes that work, exercise by exercise

From the workshop's facilitator notes. Use one at a time.

## 0 · Your idea
- Say it in one breath, as you'd say it to a friend.
- Who is the first person you'd show it to? What do they do all day?

## 1 · Write the jobs
- When does this happen to them? What does it get them?
- That sounds like a feature ("contract analysis"). When would they reach for it, and what would they have afterwards?
- What else do they try to get done around this?

## 2 · Pick the job that pays
- Who would pay to have this done, today? Name them.
- What do they do instead now, and what does that cost them?
- If none of these would pay today, which comes closest? (That is a finding in itself.)

## 3 · Features and the journey
- What does the product do to get this job done?
- What's the very first thing they do? And then what happens?
- Is that the end, or one step short? The journey ends at the job's "so", often one step past the AI's answer.

## 4 · Draft stage 1 and size it
- Which features does the journey need? Everything else, which column?
- (You draft the pieces.) Does this count look right? Which pieces do two features share?
- A draft that's too big is expected. Don't argue yet.

## 5 · Does it stand alone?
- Walk it as the customer, touching only stage 1. Where do you get stuck?
- What would cover this step? What does it cost in pieces?
- Is there a stage-1 feature no step touches? It's a candidate to cut.

## 6 · Argue it down (you are the visiting table)
- Would they still pay without this?
- "Every app has accounts." Does this one need them on day one?
- That saves N pieces. Keep it or move it? Your call.

## 7 · Write stage 1's stories
- As a…, I want to…, so that…: what's the story for this step?
- What would you see on screen if it worked?
- "Works well" can't be checked. What would you look at to know?

## 8 · Write the brief
- Which three real examples will you test with after every change? Make one of them nasty.
- Five or six features: small enough to build in a day?
- This one's an assumption. How would you find out, and what result would change your mind?
