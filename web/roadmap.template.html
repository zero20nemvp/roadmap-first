The roadmap for

GROUND ZERO’S JOB
Steps 1–3 The trace

One column per problem: what your customer does about it today, and the job hiding in that workaround. A workaround someone already pays for is the strongest proof there is.

Steps 3–4 The wall

Yellow notes are the jobs your customer is really trying to get done; blue notes are what the product does for each. The red dot is the job Ground Zero gets done first. Greyed notes come in a later release.

Steps 5–6 Size it in stories

A story is one thing someone can do, with a check that says it works. Ground Zero takes only the stories its features need; everything else waits for the release that needs it.

Steps 2 + 6 Would they pay?

Would they pay for it?

A workaround someone already pays for is the job in disguise, and the best proof they’d pay you instead. Each release is asked: does it get a job done that they pay for today?

Steps 5–6 Stories and releases

Flight plan

WaypointRelease (job completed)ETAStatus

Every release completes at least one job end to end. It need not ship every feature for that job, but the job must be doable.

Releases stack. Each builds on the one before and needs it to exist.

Target months, not dates. Delayed means moved: honest, not failure.

Grandfather pricing. Join at a release and keep that price forever; cancel and you lose it.

Step 5 Ground Zero’s stories

One story at a time, each with “done when”

Stories come from the features Ground Zero needs, and nothing else. On build day each one is one thing to build, and its “done when” is the check.
